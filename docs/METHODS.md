# Optimization methods

All methods share the same structured Q4/H8 finite-element model, supports, loads, and linear-elastic material. The application reports strain energy as C (half the usual compliance). Each history frame contains the updated design and the energy and sensitivity from the analysis preceding that update.

- **BESO soft kill:** the existing binary soft-kill method, unchanged. Void density is 0.001 and stiffness is density raised to the penalty; spatial filtering and sensitivity-history averaging allow material removal and reintroduction.
- **BESO hard kill:** solid/void densities are exactly 1/0. Void elements contribute no stiffness; inactive node DOFs are removed from the linear system. Solid energy is averaged onto shared nodes and extrapolated to void elements before spatial/history filtering. A connectivity safeguard retains minimum-cost paths from loaded cells to the fixed face, then ranks material on the connected frontier. This avoids floating pieces and point hinges; it is an additional constraint, so hard- and soft-kill designs need not match. Extremely small budgets may be exceeded by the mandatory load paths and will not be labeled converged.
- **SIMP:** continuous design densities, a row-normalized physical density filter, its transpose for the sensitivity chain rule, and an optimality-criteria update with a move limit. The volume constraint applies to physical densities after filtering.
- **ESO:** unidirectional removal ranked by filtered element energy sensitivity. Removed elements cannot return. Integer element counts round the volume up, with at most one element of discrepancy. A final unchanged analysis evaluates the completed design.
- **Level set:** Hamilton–Jacobi transport of a cell-centered signed-distance field, positive inside solid. Initial circular holes (through-holes in 3D) provide boundaries to evolve. The normal speed uses solid-side elastic energy extended into the void and filtered, a volume multiplier, and curvature smoothing. Godunov upwind differences and a CFL step limit advance the field. Every five steps, nearest interpolated zero crossings rebuild distance, followed by a volume-preserving shift. At fixed volume, actual FEA energy checks backtrack uphill trials. This is a shape-driven teaching discretization inspired by [Allaire, Jouve & Toader (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2003.09.032), not a reproduction of their code. It permits hole merging/closure but has no separate hole-nucleation step.

Level set uses linear ersatz stiffness with a 1e-6 void stiffness ratio and a smooth Heaviside transition confined to ±0.75 element lengths. Its exact discrete energy sensitivity is `-H'(phi) * unitEnergy`. The 2D viewer interpolates the zero contour; 3D uses a voxel approximation. Displayed solid fraction can differ from the narrow-band integration used for volume and energy. SIMP shows continuous density in 2D and a 0.5 threshold surface in 3D.

The level-set volume schedule is limited by the admissible boundary motion. Reaching the iteration limit or rejecting a trial is not convergence. Initial holes, mesh, filter radius, and step size affect the local optimum. Fixed-volume line search can stall; this is reported as an iteration limit rather than falsely claiming convergence.

## Checks

Run `scripts/verify.sh` for numerical checks, including the SIMP filtered-gradient finite-difference test, 2D/3D volume and density bounds, ESO removal monotonicity, hard-kill equality with an independently reduced mesh, level-set adjoint finite differences, planar interface advection, signed-distance reconstruction, and the field/density relation. With a server running, `python3 tests/api_methods.py` checks method dispatch and serialized histories; `python3 tests/api_smoke.py` checks pause, step, resume, stop, and input validation. Container checks run both suites against the published image.
