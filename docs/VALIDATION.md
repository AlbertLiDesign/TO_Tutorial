# Verification scope

Run `scripts/verify.sh` for the local numerical suites. Container CI runs the corresponding checks against the published image and archives its reports. `tests/api_methods.py` and `tests/api_smoke.py` exercise HTTP dispatch, serialization and lifecycle controls.

## Independent evidence

| Component | Evidence | What this does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Q4/H8 elasticity | Symmetry, rigid modes, analytic constant-strain energy, repeated integration; load/modulus/length scaling | Nonlinear, buckling, fracture or experimental validity |
| Frame objective | Reanalysis of displayed density; compliance checked against external work for every method | Equality with old pre-update strain-energy exports |
| SIMP | Filtered finite differences; 20-step 2D/3D density and compliance comparison against independently assembled SciPy/Gauss/Brent fixtures | Execution of the original MATLAB program or global optimality |
| Soft-kill BESO | Existing trajectory/density regression and independent model assembly checks | Universal agreement across every mesh, compiler or solver platform |
| Hard-kill BESO | Exact comparison with an independently reduced mesh; binary densities; actual material reintroduction; same-state work equality | Guaranteed connectivity or a published full optimization trajectory |
| ESO | No regrowth, volume quantization, positive finite analysis and state consistency | Equivalence to the original stress-rejection ESO variant |
| Level set | Discrete energy derivative finite differences, analytic planar transport, planar signed-distance reconstruction, volume and accepted fixed-volume descent checks | Validation of the complete shape gradient against a published boundary-integral result, or reproduction of an entire paper's optimization trajectory |

The independent SIMP fixture is generated by original code in `tests/reference/generate_simp.py`. It uses two-point Gauss integration, SciPy sparse assembly/solve, and Brent root finding for OC volume, instead of the application’s element formula, solver and bisection. Its equations follow the density-filter formulation documented by Andreassen et al. (2011). NumPy/SciPy are only needed to regenerate the fixture; normal application and container execution do not require them.

Acceptance tolerances and exercised settings are in `server/MethodVerification.cs`, not inferred from screenshots. Detailed results are written to `artifacts/methods-report.json`. Tests intentionally check failure conditions such as invalid settings; a failed mechanism must not be labeled converged or returned with invented energy.

## Remaining work before a broader certification claim

- Reproduce externally published complete optimization trajectories for hard-kill BESO, the selected ESO variant and level set, with precisely matched boundary conditions, initialization, discretization and stopping criteria.
- Check the level-set boundary velocity against an independent shape-derivative calculation, in addition to the existing derivative of its discrete Heaviside material model. Those are different checks.
- Conduct mesh-refinement studies at fixed physical geometry and filter radius, including disconnected and nearly singular designs.
- Obtain independent technical review of both language versions of the Tutorial.

The application implements identifiable classical formulations, but these tests do not justify “error-free,” “certified,” or universal reference-equivalence claims. The Tutorial makes that distinction explicit.
